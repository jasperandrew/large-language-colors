## Large Language Colors: Variation and Misalignment in LLM Color Naming

Jasper Andrew, Anna Feldman

Montclair State University

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed in contexts requiring perceptual and culturally-grounded language, yet little is known about how closely they align with color naming behavior in humans. This study presents a systematic sampling of color term elicitations across the RGB color space, comparing two LLMs (GPT-4.5 mini and Gemini 2.5 Flash, hereon referred to as GPT and Gemini respectively) against human baseline data from the Many Languages, Many Colors (MLMC) project. Swatches distributed across the RGB cube were presented to each model, and responses were normalized to canonical color terms for analysis. Results reveal that both models diverge from human color term patterns: whereas human participants produce gradual, overlapping category boundaries reflecting the organic ambiguity of perceptual experience, both LLMs exhibit sharper, more rigid boundaries that fail to capture this natural variability. This misalignment suggests that LLMs do not closely reflect human color naming characteristics, with potential consequences for applications requiring human-perspective alignment. Beyond their shared divergence from human data, the two models differ markedly from each other: ChatGPT produces a narrower color term repertoire than Gemini, indicating meaningful variation in how foundation models encode perceptual language. Gemini also demonstrates limited sensitivity to linguistic context, producing partially distinct term distributions between Chinese and English prompts, a pattern which appears largely absent in ChatGPT. These findings raise important questions about the cultural representational accuracy of LLMs in multilingual and cross-cultural applications, and call for broader evaluation of perceptual language grounding in foundation models.
